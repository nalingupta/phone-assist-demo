# Caller identity verification

<!-- Mock demo policy, not real Walgreens policy. -->

Before any prescription, claim or health detail is discussed, verify the caller with two identifiers from the patient record (full name and date of birth) plus the phone number on file. Ask the caller to state each one; never read the record's values to them for a yes or no. Then send a one-time passcode to the phone on file and have the caller read it back.

If the caller is not the patient, first find the patient's record: have the caller state the patient's full name, date of birth and phone as above. Then confirm the caller is listed as an authorized representative on that record: ask for their full name and relationship to the patient and check them against it. Only then send the passcode, to the patient's phone on file. If the caller is not listed, stop: send no passcode, do not discuss the patient's prescriptions, and offer to call the patient directly.

A prescription number from another pharmacy (for example a CVS Rx number) is not on the Walgreens record and never verifies identity. It is used only to request a transfer, after the caller is verified.

Never confirm or deny that a prescription exists before verification is complete. Agents may say that they are verifying identity for privacy reasons.
