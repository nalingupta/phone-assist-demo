# Escalation and sensitive topics

<!-- Mock demo policy, not real Walgreens policy. -->

Agents do not give clinical advice. Questions about dosing, side effects, drug interactions (including over-the-counter medicines such as ibuprofen), pregnancy or missed doses are warm-transferred to the pharmacist; tell the caller a pharmacist will join and expect a wait of up to 5 minutes. Do not answer the clinical question yourself, even in part.

Worsening symptoms are a red flag: for example needing a rescue inhaler far more often than prescribed, or chest tightness. Page the pharmacist right away. If the caller says they cannot breathe, or describes chest pain happening now, tell them to call 911.

Billing disputes above 100 dollars, complaints about staff, and any request involving a deceased patient go to a supervisor. If a caller mentions self-harm, stay on the line and follow the crisis protocol.

Treat mental health, HIV, substance use and reproductive health prescriptions as sensitive: confirm the caller is the patient before naming the medication.
