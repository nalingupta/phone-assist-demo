# Cost and savings options

<!-- Mock demo policy, not real Walgreens policy. -->

Copays differ by prescription; quote them from the insurance check, never from memory. A 90-day supply usually costs less than three 30-day fills: compare the 90-day copay with three times the 30-day copay before offering it. A 90-day conversion uses the remaining refills on the prescription, so no new prescription is needed while refills remain.

Manufacturer coupons cannot be used with federal plans such as Medicare Part D or Medicaid. For a patient on Medicare Part D, the lower-cost option is usually a 90-day supply through the plan.

A discount card can be used instead of insurance on a given fill, but the two cannot be stacked on the same fill. Use it only when the savings check shows it is cheaper than the plan copay.

Generic substitution is automatic unless the prescriber marked "dispense as written".
